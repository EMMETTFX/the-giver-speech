Task 1 • About the Book

Setting

  • A future community built on Sameness
  • Families and jobs are assigned
  • Almost no color, weather, or real choice
  • Other communities exist beyond its border
Task 1 • About the Book

The Conflict

Jonas's job

Chosen as Receiver of Memory. Holds memories the community erased.

What changes

He feels pain, joy, and isolation. He can no longer accept Sameness.

The real conflict

Jonas vs. the community's control. Stay in safety, or leave for a real life.

Task 2

Best Part: The First Memory

  • The Giver gives Jonas snow
  • Jonas feels cold, speed, and joy
  • The community had erased weather itself
Task 3

Life Lesson

A life with no pain, memory, or choice also has no real joy.

Memories matter

Without the past, people cannot understand love, loss, or truth.

Choice is human

Sameness keeps people safe, but it also takes away who they are.

Example

Jonas learns that “release” is not peaceful. It is the community hiding death.

Task 4

What Makes It Unique

Quiet dystopia

Not a ruined high-tech future. The streets look calm.

No usual disasters

No hunger, crime, or war in the community.

The real danger

Control and Sameness hide under the word “safe.”

Task 4

Text Features

Memory sequences

When a memory is passed on, the page becomes vivid, like you are inside it.

Third-person focus

The story is told in third person, but it stays close to Jonas.

Graphic-novel tools

Panels, faces, and black-and-white art show Sameness. Color breaks it.

Symbols

Sled = freedom. Red = feeling. Apple = the first crack in Sameness. Gabriel = the reason Jonas leaves.

Closing

Recommendation

  • Jonas leaves a “perfect” life for a real one.
  • Freedom and feelings are worth the pain.
  • I recommend this book because it makes you think about choice.
  • It is a strong pick for readers who like thoughtful dystopian stories.
